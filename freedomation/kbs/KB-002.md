## Purpose

Map prospects to resonant persona archetypes based on observable behavior and patterns. This enables targeted value offers and appropriate messaging tone without inventing personal details.

---

## Inputs

- **artifact.project_brief.v0_1**: Campaign context and target hints
- **artifact.prospect_list.v0_1**: List of prospects with basic info

---

## Outputs

- **artifact.persona_map.v0_1**: Persona archetype assignments
- **artifact.prospect_snapshot.v0_1**: Enriched prospect profiles (optional, for deeper research)

---

## Process

### 1. Gather Observable Data

For each prospect, collect only verifiable facts from public sources:
- LinkedIn activity (posts, comments, job changes)
- Twitter/X posts and engagement patterns
- Published articles or talks
- Company news and funding announcements
- GitHub activity (for technical roles)
- Podcast appearances or interviews

**Rule:** Never assume or invent. If you don't have data, note "unknown" instead of guessing.

### 2. Identify Behavioral Patterns

Look for signals that suggest persona archetype:

**The Ambitious Builder**
- Signals: Rapid career progression, talks about scaling/growth, shares wins publicly
- Motivation: Impact and achievement
- Communication style: Direct, results-oriented

**The Careful Optimizer**
- Signals: Shares data/metrics, focuses on efficiency, asks detailed questions
- Motivation: Control and optimization
- Communication style: Analytical, thorough

**The Visionary Explorer**
- Signals: Shares thought leadership, experiments publicly, early adopter
- Motivation: Innovation and discovery
- Communication style: Big-picture, conceptual

**The Collaborative Connector**
- Signals: Amplifies others, makes intros, builds community
- Motivation: Relationships and belonging
- Communication style: Warm, collaborative

### 3. Map to Persona Archetype

Assign primary persona based on strongest signal pattern. Note secondary patterns if relevant.

**Template:**
```yaml
persona_archetype: "The Ambitious Builder"
core_motivation: "Growth and impact"
communication_preference: "Direct and data-driven"
likely_challenges:
  - "Scaling team while maintaining culture"
  - "Finding technical talent"
resonant_themes: ["autonomy", "impact", "efficiency"]
```

### 4. Define Messaging Approach

Based on persona, determine:
- **Tone:** Direct vs warm, formal vs casual
- **Value angle:** What kind of outcome resonates
- **Asset type:** Guide vs template vs case study
- **CTA style:** Calendar link vs open-ended question

### 5. Document Anti-Patterns

Note what to **avoid** for this persona:
- Ambitious Builder → Avoid vague promises, focus on specifics
- Careful Optimizer → Avoid hype, provide data/proof
- Visionary Explorer → Avoid tactical details in opener, lead with big idea
- Collaborative Connector → Avoid transactional framing, emphasize community

## Examples

### Example 1: Mapping "Sarah Chen, VP of Engineering at TechFlow"

**Observable Data:**
- Recent LinkedIn update: "Excited to announce we've doubled the team to 40 engineers in 6 months 🚀"
- Posted on Twitter: "The hardest part of hypergrowth isn't hiring, it's maintaining quality and culture"
- Company news: Raised $15M Series B 2 months ago
- Speaking at upcoming conference: "Scaling Engineering Teams in High-Growth SaaS"

**Persona Assignment:**
- **Primary archetype:** The Ambitious Builder
- **Core motivation:** Impact and achievement (rapidly scaling)
- **Likely challenges:** Maintaining culture during hypergrowth, onboarding engineers quickly
- **Communication preference:** Direct, results-oriented (based on speaking topics)

**Messaging Approach:**
- **Tone:** Professional but warm, get to the point
- **Value angle:** "Help you onboard engineers 50% faster" (specific outcome)
- **Asset type:** Template or framework (actionable, not theoretical)
- **CTA:** "I'd love to send you our onboarding framework—would that be helpful?"

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**
- ❌ Vague promises ("Transform your engineering culture")
- ❌ Overly long explanation (she's busy scaling)
- ❌ Generic pitch (this is very specific to her situation)

### Example 2: Mapping "Michael Torres, Director of Product"

**Observable Data:**
- LinkedIn: Shares articles about product metrics and A/B testing
- Recent post: "Our new analytics dashboard reduced decision time from 3 days to 3 hours"
- Twitter: Mostly retweets of data visualization content
- No major company news found

**Persona Assignment:**
- **Primary archetype:** The Careful Optimizer
- **Core motivation:** Efficiency and control
- **Likely challenges:** Data quality, tool fragmentation, proving ROI
- **Communication preference:** Analytical, detail-oriented

**Messaging Approach:**
- **Tone:** Professional, data-forward
- **Value angle:** "Save X hours per week" or "Improve Y metric by Z%"
- **Asset type:** Case study with real data, or comparison spreadsheet
- **CTA:** "I put together a comparison of 5 analytics tools with pricing + feature matrix. Want me to send it?"

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**
- ❌ Hype or superlatives ("Amazing game-changing tool!")
- ❌ Lack of specifics (provide numbers, not platitudes)
- ❌ Skipping proof (he'll want to see evidence)

## Edge Cases & Warnings

### Warning 1: Mixed Signals
Some prospects show signals for multiple personas. Choose the **strongest** pattern, note secondary. Don't over-complicate.

### Warning 2: Limited Data
If prospect has minimal public presence:
- Default to neutral/professional tone
- Use more generic value framing
- Don't guess or assume

### Warning 3: Changing Personas
Personas can shift with role changes. A Director who becomes VP may shift from "Careful Optimizer" to "Ambitious Builder." Check recent activity.

### Warning 4: Avoid Stereotyping
Personas are **patterns**, not personality tests. Don't treat them as rigid boxes. A "Careful Optimizer" can still appreciate big vision.
