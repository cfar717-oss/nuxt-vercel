## Purpose

Guide the transition from one-on-one outbound conversations to ongoing community relationships. Turn resonant prospects into community members, collaborators, and long-term connections.

## Process

### 1. Identify Resonance Threshold

Don't invite everyone to community. Wait for strong resonance signals:

**Strong Signals (Invite-Ready):**
- Asked multiple thoughtful questions
- Shared their own challenge or context
- Engaged with 2+ touchpoints
- Requested introduction or referral
- Expressed explicit interest in staying connected

**Weak Signals (Not Yet):**
- Polite one-word replies
- Downloaded asset but no follow-up
- Opened messages but didn't engage

**Rule:** Invite after 2-3 high-quality exchanges, not after first reply.

### 2. Craft Permission-Based Community Invitation

**Structure:**
```
1. Acknowledge the conversation ("We've been exchanging thoughts on X...")
2. Context for community ("I run a small group of [persona] navigating [challenge]")
3. Value proposition ("What you get: [specific benefits]")
4. Permission framing ("If that sounds valuable, you're welcome to join—no pressure")
5. Easy opt-in ("Just reply 'interested' and I'll send the link")
6. What happens next ("I'll send Slack invite within 24 hours")
```

**Example:**
```
Hi Sarah,

We've been exchanging thoughts on scaling eng teams—really enjoyed your insights on maintaining culture during hypergrowth.

I run a small community of SaaS VPs navigating exactly this stage (Series A-B, 20-100 eng). We share what's working, what's not, and occasionally do group problem-solving sessions.

What you get:
- Direct Q&A with people 6 months ahead of you
- Our shared "hypergrowth playbook" (living doc)
- Monthly deep-dives on topics like hiring, onboarding, culture

It's low-key (no spam, no pitches), and you can leave anytime.

If that sounds valuable, you're welcome to join—just reply "interested" and I'll send the Slack invite.

Either way, thanks for the great conversation!
```

### 3. Design Onboarding Flow

**First 24 Hours:**
- Welcome message (personal, not automated)
- Quick orientation (channel guide, norms)
- Introduce to 1-2 specific people they'd resonate with
- Ask a lightweight question to spark engagement

**First Week:**
- Share 1-2 highly relevant resources
- Invite to first async discussion or event
- Check in: "How's it going so far? Anything you'd like to see more of?"

**First Month:**
- Facilitate first meaningful contribution
- Introduce to broader group
- Gather feedback on experience

### 4. Maintain Resonance (Don't Let It Die)

**Common Community Failure Modes:**
- Too much activity (spam, overwhelming)
- Too little activity (ghost town)
- No clear purpose (vague "networking")
- All broadcast, no dialogue

**Resonant Community Practices:**

**Curate, Don't Spam:**
- 2-3 high-quality posts/week > 20 low-quality
- Every share should answer "Why does this matter to us?"

**Facilitate, Don't Dominate:**
- Pose questions that spark peer-to-peer dialogue
- Highlight member contributions
- Create space for others to lead

**Celebrate Wins:**
- Share member successes (with permission)
- Recognize helpful contributors
- Build a culture of mutual support

**Maintain Boundaries:**
- No self-promotion without context/value
- No spam or irrelevant content
- Enforce norms consistently

### 5. Community Invitation Timing

**Touchpoint 1 (Initial Outreach):** Don't mention community
**Touchpoint 2-3 (Engagement):** If strong resonance, mention community exists
**Touchpoint 4-5 (Deep Conversation):** Extend explicit invitation

**Example Timeline:**
- Day 0: Initial outreach with value offer
- Day 3: Follow-up, they ask question (strong signal)
- Day 7: Answer their question, mention community in passing
- Day 10: Continued dialogue, extend formal invitation

## Examples

### Example 1: Moving from DM to Community

**Context:** You've had 3 exchanges with a VP of Eng about scaling challenges.

**Invitation Message:**
```
Hi David,

I've really enjoyed our exchanges on eng scaling—your approach to manager training is smart.

Quick question: I'm building a small community of VPs navigating this exact stage (hypergrowth, 20-100 eng). We do monthly problem-solving sessions and maintain a shared playbook.

Current members: 12 VPs from Series A-B SaaS companies. Mix of first-time and repeat leaders.

If you're interested in joining, I'd love to have you. Just a low-key Slack group (no spam, you can leave anytime).

Reply "interested" and I'll send the invite. No worries if it's not your thing!

— Casey
```

**Why this works:**
- Builds on existing conversation
- Specific about who's there and what you do
- Permission-based ("no worries if not")
- Easy opt-in

### Example 2: Community Onboarding Message

**New Member (Sarah) Just Joined:**

**Welcome DM:**
```
Hey Sarah!

Welcome to the SaaS Eng Leaders group 👋

Quick orientation:
- #wins: Share your team wins (we celebrate here)
- #challenges: Ask for advice (no judgment, all of us struggle)
- #resources: Best articles/tools/frameworks we've found
- #random: Non-work chat

Two people you should meet:
- @alex (also scaling from 30→80 eng right now)
- @jordan (went through Series B scaling 2 years ago, great mentor)

One question to get you started: What's the #1 thing keeping you up at night re: your team right now?

Glad to have you here!
```

**Follow-Up (Day 3):**
```
Sarah, saw your question in #challenges about onboarding timelines—great topic.

@alex and @jordan both have frameworks for this. Alex's 30/60/90 template is in #resources (pinned). Jordan ran a workshop on this last month (recording in thread).

Let me know if you want me to intro you to either directly!
```

## Edge Cases & Warnings

### Warning 1: Don't Invite Too Early
Inviting after one message feels pushy:
- ❌ "Thanks for downloading the guide! Join our community here: [link]"
- ✅ Wait for 2-3 quality exchanges first

### Warning 2: Set Realistic Expectations
Don't oversell community value:
- ❌ "This community will change your life!"
- ✅ "Low-key group where we share what's working and ask for advice"

### Warning 3: Communities Require Work
Don't start a community if you can't maintain it:
- Minimum: 3-5 hours/week for curation + facilitation
- Scaling: Need mods/helpers once you hit 50-100 members
- Alternative: Curated newsletter (less work than community)

### Warning 4: Quality > Size
Don't optimize for member count:
- 20 highly-engaged members > 200 lurkers
- Screen for fit (not everyone should join)
- It's okay to have a small, tight-knit community

---

## Quality Checks

Before inviting someone to community:

- [ ] Had 2-3 high-quality exchanges (not just asset download)
- [ ] They've shown strong resonance signals (questions, context-sharing)
- [ ] They're a good fit for community (persona, stage, values)
- [ ] Invitation is permission-based (no pressure)
- [ ] You've set realistic expectations (what community is/isn't)
- [ ] Onboarding plan ready (not throwing them into the deep end)

For maintaining community health:

- [ ] Clear purpose and norms
- [ ] Consistent moderation
- [ ] Regular valuable content (2-3x/week)
- [ ] Peer-to-peer dialogue facilitated
- [ ] Member contributions celebrated
- [ ] No spam or self-promotion without value
