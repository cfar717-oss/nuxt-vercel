# KB-003: Trauma-Informed Messaging Guidelines

**Version:** 0.1.0
**Status:** draft
**Layer:** L2
**Type:** procedural
**Category:** content-creation
**Credibility:** medium
**Risk:** high

---

## Purpose

Guide the creation of outbound messaging that is supportive and empathetic **without** being clinical, therapeutic, or manipulative. Avoid shame, pressure, and fear-based tactics while maintaining warmth and authenticity.

---

## Inputs

- Draft outreach message (email, DM, invitation)
- Target persona archetype (from KB-002)

---

## Outputs

- Revised message that is supportive without being therapeutic
- List of avoided trauma triggers and manipulation tactics

---

## Process

### 1. Identify and Remove Shame-Based Language

**Shame triggers include:**
- "If you're still struggling with..."
- "Stop making these mistakes..."
- "Are you tired of failing at..."
- "Don't be the person who..."
- Winner/loser dichotomies ("Successful people do X, unsuccessful people do Y")

**Replace with supportive framing:**
- "Many people find [challenge] difficult. Here's an approach..."
- "If [challenge] resonates with you, this might help."
- "This is designed for people navigating [challenge]."

### 2. Avoid Fear-Based Motivation

**Fear triggers include:**
- "You're losing money every day without..."
- "Your competitors are doing this and you're not"
- "If you don't act now, you'll miss..."
- "What you don't know is costing you..."

**Replace with opportunity framing:**
- "Here's an approach that's worked for teams like yours."
- "If you're curious about [outcome], this might be helpful."
- "I've found a pattern that tends to help with [challenge]."

### 3. Remove Therapy/Clinical Language

**Clinical framing to avoid:**
- "Heal your trauma/wounds"
- "Release your limiting beliefs"
- "Transform your deep-seated patterns"
- "Breakthrough your blocks"
- "Unpack your feelings"

**Replace with practical language:**
- "Navigate challenges" (not "heal trauma")
- "Shift perspective" (not "release limiting beliefs")
- "Try a different approach" (not "transform patterns")
- "Work through obstacles" (not "breakthrough blocks")

### 4. Ensure Permission-Based CTAs

**Pressure CTAs to avoid:**
- "Claim your spot now"
- "You need to act fast"
- "You'll regret missing this"
- "Secure your place before it's gone"

**Permission-based alternatives:**
- "If this resonates, you're welcome to explore..."
- "When you're ready, here's how to connect..."
- "I'd love to share more if you're interested."
- "No pressure—just wanted to offer this resource."

### 5. Validate Without Diagnosing

**It's okay to:**
- Acknowledge common challenges
- Normalize difficulty
- Express empathy
- Offer support

**It's NOT okay to:**
- Diagnose mental health conditions
- Claim to treat psychological issues
- Position your service as therapy
- Use clinical assessment language

## Examples

### Example 1: Rewriting a Shame-Based Opener

**Before (Shame-based):**
> Are you still struggling to retain customers? Most founders fail because they don't understand the psychology of churn. Don't be one of them.

**After (Supportive):**
> Customer retention is one of the trickiest challenges for early-stage SaaS teams. If this is something you're navigating, I've put together a framework that might help.

**What changed:**
- Removed "struggling" and "fail" (shame triggers)
- Removed "Don't be one of them" (fear-based)
- Normalized the challenge ("trickiest challenges")
- Made offer permission-based ("might help")

### Example 2: Removing Clinical Language

**Before (Clinical):**
> It's time to heal your relationship with sales and release the limiting beliefs that are blocking your revenue growth. This transformational program will help you breakthrough your money blocks.

**After (Practical):**
> Many founders feel uncomfortable with outbound sales—it's a common challenge. This program focuses on practical frameworks for starting conversations without feeling pushy. If that resonates, I'd love to share the approach.

**What changed:**
- Removed therapy language ("heal", "release limiting beliefs", "breakthrough blocks")
- Focused on practical outcome ("practical frameworks")
- Normalized the feeling ("common challenge")
- Added permission-based invitation

### Example 3: Converting Fear to Opportunity

**Before (Fear-based):**
> WARNING: Your competitors are using AI and you're falling behind. Every day you wait is costing you market share. Don't let them win.

**After (Opportunity-based):**
> AI implementation is becoming table stakes for SaaS companies. If you're curious about how to approach it without a massive investment, I put together a roadmap based on 10 implementations.

**What changed:**
- Removed competitive fear ("competitors", "falling behind", "don't let them win")
- Removed urgency pressure ("every day you wait")
- Framed as curiosity and opportunity
- Offered specific value (roadmap)

## Edge Cases & Warnings

### Warning 1: Supportive ≠ Therapy
You can be warm and empathetic without being a therapist. Stay in your lane:
- ✅ "This challenge is common for people in your stage"
- ❌ "Let's explore the emotional roots of your resistance to sales"

### Warning 2: Don't Swing Too Far
Removing manipulation doesn't mean being bland:
- ✅ Direct and clear: "I'd love to send you the guide—would that be helpful?"
- ❌ Overly passive: "Maybe you might possibly be interested, no pressure though?"

### Warning 3: Context Matters
B2B tech outreach has different norms than wellness/coaching. Adjust warmth level:
- B2B SaaS → Professional warmth
- Coaching/Wellness → More warmth (but still no therapy claims)

### Warning 4: Real Trauma Awareness
If your audience genuinely deals with trauma (e.g., burnout support for founders), be extra careful:
- Acknowledge reality without exploiting it
- Provide resources, don't position yourself as healer
- Partner with actual therapists if appropriate

---

## Quality Checks

Run every message through these checks before sending:

- [ ] No shame-based language ("still struggling", "failing", "mistakes")
- [ ] No fear-based motivation ("losing money", "falling behind", "competitors winning")
- [ ] No clinical/therapy language ("heal", "release beliefs", "transform trauma")
- [ ] CTAs are permission-based invitations, not demands
- [ ] Challenges are normalized, not weaponized
- [ ] Tone is supportive but not therapeutic
- [ ] Would I appreciate receiving this message?
