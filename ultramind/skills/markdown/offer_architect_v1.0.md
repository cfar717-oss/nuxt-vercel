## FRAMEWORKS

### Framework 1: UMP/UMS Architecture

**Definition:**
- **UMP** = named hidden reason other solutions fail (the villain)
- **UMS** = proprietary named way the offer defeats that villain (the hero)

**UMP Requirements:**
- ✅ Specific + named syndrome/effect/crisis
- ✅ Explains systemic failure (not "it's hard")
- ✅ Feels like revelation ("aha")
- ✅ Creates a villain (real enemy)
- ✅ Validates past failures ("no wonder")

**UMS Requirements:**
- ✅ Proprietary name (feels owned)
- ✅ Directly addresses UMP
- ✅ Sounds credible (not fantasy)
- ✅ Teachable (clear explanation)
- ✅ Structured framework (phases/components)

**Relationship:**
```
UMP → UMS → PROOF → OFFER
```

**Key Insight:** Without UMP/UMS the offer is generic. With UMP/UMS it is unique and differentiated.

---

### Framework 2: The 6 Positioning Angles

#### ANGLE 1: Contrarian
- **Position:** "Everyone's doing it wrong"
- **Structure:** Common approach → why it fails (unexpected) → better way (UMS) → proof
- **Best For:** Educated, skeptical audiences
- **Risk:** Must prove contrarian claim

#### ANGLE 2: Discovery / Weird Revelation
- **Position:** "I stumbled on something strange (but credible)"
- **Structure:** Discovery story → weird-but-effective → logical explanation → results
- **Best For:** Curiosity-driven audiences
- **Risk:** Cannot feel scammy "weird trick"

#### ANGLE 3: Protective Education / Whistleblower
- **Position:** "Industry doesn't want you to know"
- **Structure:** Industry standard → hidden problem → why hidden (profit motive) → alternative (UMS)
- **Best For:** Burned-before, skeptical audiences
- **Risk:** Must be truthful; avoid conspiracy tone

#### ANGLE 4: Authority / Expert
- **Position:** "After years studying this..."
- **Structure:** Authority → deep insight → what others miss → proprietary system (UMS)
- **Best For:** Technical/scientific products
- **Risk:** Needs legitimate authority

#### ANGLE 5: Transformation Story
- **Position:** "I was just like you—then I discovered..."
- **Structure:** Relatable struggle → low point → UMP discovery → UMS breakthrough → transformation
- **Best For:** Personal brands, coaching
- **Risk:** Must be authentic

#### ANGLE 6: Category Creation
- **Position:** "This isn't X, it's Y"
- **Structure:** Old category limits → new category → why superior → UMS as foundation
- **Best For:** Innovative products, premium positioning
- **Risk:** Requires education on new category

**Selection Criteria:**
1. Market sophistication (how many solutions tried)
2. Your credibility (authority/story)
3. True uniqueness (product vs positioning)
4. Audience psychology (what resonates)

### Framework 3: Differentiation Strategy

**5 Differentiation Dimensions:**

1. **Mechanism Differentiation**
   - Differentiate HOW it works, not THAT it works

2. **Ingredient/Component Differentiation**
   - Specific, provable superiority (not "quality")

3. **Delivery/Experience Differentiation**
   - Ecosystem vs isolated product

4. **Philosophical Differentiation**
   - Values and operating principles

5. **Proof/Credibility Differentiation**
   - Specific, verifiable proof

**Competitive Positioning Matrix:**
Use a matrix to compare:
- Mechanism
- Time
- Approach
- Risk
- Cost

vs competitors/alternatives

### Framework 4: Value Proposition Architecture

**5 Layers (Must Align & Reinforce):**

**Layer 1: Core Promise**
- One sentence transformation

**Layer 2: Unique Mechanism**
- Why this works when others fail (UMP/UMS)

**Layer 3: Proof Points**
- 3–5 concrete evidence bullets

**Layer 4: Differentiation**
- Comparison vs alternatives

**Layer 5: Transformation Vision**
- Aspirational identity shift

---

### Framework 5: Proof Requirement Architecture

**Rule:** For each claim in UMP/UMS, define:
- Required proof type
- Specifics
- Strength
- Accessibility

**Proof Strength Hierarchy (9 Tiers):**

**Tier 1** (Strongest): Third-party verified data
**Tier 2**: Before/after measurements
**Tier 3**: Specific testimonials (names/dates/numbers)
**Tier 4**: Expert endorsements
**Tier 5**: Mechanism explanations (reasoning + science)
**Tier 6**: Comparative data
**Tier 7**: Generic testimonials
**Tier 8**: Unsubstantiated hype claims
**Tier 9** (Weakest): Vague benefits

**Your Job:** Define what proof is needed and where to get it.

## EXECUTION PROCESS (8 Phases)

### Phase 1: Discovery & Research (60-90 min)

**Inputs:**
- Product/service overview
- Target audience/avatar
- Current market position
- Competitive landscape
- Existing messaging (if any)

**Research Questions:**

**About the Offer:**
- What problem does it solve (specific)?
- How does it work (mechanism)?
- What makes it different (real)?
- What results (specific outcomes)?
- Who has it worked for (proof)?

**About the Market:**
- What solutions do people try first?
- Why do they fail?
- What do competitors claim?
- Market sophistication level?
- Skepticism level?

**About the Audience:**
- What have they tried before?
- Why didn't it work?
- What do they believe about the problem?
- What makes them believe change is possible?
- What proof do they need?

---

### Phase 2: UMP Development (30-45 min)

**Steps:**
1. List 3–5 common solutions and why each fails
2. Find common thread: the hidden mechanism they all miss
3. Name it (credible, explanatory, memorable, revelation)
4. Explain simply (2–3 sentences)
5. Run "explain to a friend" test

---

### Phase 3: UMS Development (30-45 min)

**Steps:**
1. Define how the offer specifically addresses the UMP
2. Create proprietary framework (phases/components) with a name
3. Make UMS→UMP connection explicit ("Because the problem is…")
4. Validate:
   - Uniqueness
   - Explainability
   - Proprietary feel
   - Provability

---

### Phase 4: Angle Selection (20-30 min)

**Method:**
1. Score each of 6 angles for fit:
   - Insight
   - Proof
   - Audience receptivity
   - Authenticity
2. Select highest fit score + authentic
3. Build narrative:
   - Opening
   - Build
   - Reveal (UMP/UMS)
   - Proof

---

### Phase 5: Differentiation Strategy (30-45 min)

**Steps:**
1. List 3–5 competitors/alternatives:
   - Their approach
   - Weakness
   - Your advantage
2. Declare unique position: "We are the only ___ that ___"
3. For each differentiation claim: define proof and proof type

---

### Phase 6: Value Proposition Assembly (20-30 min)

**Template:**
```
CORE PROMISE: [One sentence transformation]

UNIQUE MECHANISM:
- UMP: [Hidden reason others fail]
- UMS: [Proprietary solution system]

PROOF POINTS:
1. [Specific, verifiable proof]
2. [Specific, verifiable proof]
3. [Specific, verifiable proof]
4. [Specific, verifiable proof]

DIFFERENTIATION: [vs competitors/alternatives]

TRANSFORMATION VISION: [Aspirational identity shift]
```

---

### Phase 7: Messaging Hierarchy (15-20 min)

**Three Levels:**

**PRIMARY:**
- Core promise + UMP/UMS + strongest proof

**SECONDARY:**
- Benefits + differentiation + process/timeline + risk reversal

**TERTIARY:**
- FAQ/objections + technical details + pricing justification + guarantee terms

**Rule:** This hierarchy guides all downstream execution.

---

### Phase 8: Proof Requirements Specification (15-20 min)

**For Each Category:**
- **UMP Claims** → proof type → source
- **UMS Claims** → proof type → source
- **Differentiation Claims** → proof type → source
- **Results Claims** → proof type → source

**If Proof Doesn't Exist:**
- Define what can be gathered
- Timeline
- Interim proof strategy
- Proof-building plan

## DECISION RULES

### Rule 1: High Sophistication Market
**If:** Market is highly sophisticated
**Then:**
- UMP must be deep (not surface)
- Prefer contrarian or protective education
- Higher proof standards
- Differentiation must be substantial

### Rule 2: Low Sophistication Market
**If:** Market is unsophisticated
**Then:**
- UMP should be simple and educational
- Prefer authority or transformation story
- Testimonials can carry more weight
- Differentiation easier (less comparison)

### Rule 3: Commodity Product
**If:** Product is commodity
**Then:**
- UMP/UMS becomes primary differentiation lever
- Lean into delivery/experience/philosophy
- Consider category creation

### Rule 4: Innovative Product
**If:** Product is truly innovative
**Then:**
- Angle: discovery or category creation
- Proof is critical for trust
- Education load increases

### Rule 5: Burned/Skeptical Audience
**If:** Audience is burned/skeptical
**Then:**
- UMP validates past failures
- Angle: protective education or transformation story
- Proof must be specific and verifiable
- Differentiation emphasizes "why this is different"

### Rule 6: Strong Credibility/Authority
**If:** Credentials/authority are strong
**Then:**
- Angle: authority or contrarian
- UMP/UMS can be more technical
- Proof can include expertise/research
- Credibility becomes part of differentiation

## ANTI-PATTERNS (NEVER DO THIS)

### ❌ NEVER:
- **Fake uniqueness** ("revolutionary" without innovation)
- **Overly complex UMP/UMS** (fails explain-to-8th-grader test)
- **Generic claims** ("high quality," "best value," "great service")
- **Copying competitor positioning** (me-too angle)
- **Unprovable/exaggerated claims**
- **Angle mismatch** (contrarian w/o insight, authority w/o creds, discovery w/o story)

### ✅ INSTEAD:
- Find real white space or create it via mechanism/delivery/category
- Simple, teachable, credible mechanism explanations
- Specific, provable differentiators
- Truthful proof architecture before bold claims
